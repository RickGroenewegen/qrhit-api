Music bingo is the easiest party game I know to explain and the most annoying one to prepare. The rules take ten seconds: everyone gets a card with song titles on it, you play snippets, people mark off what they recognise, first full card shouts bingo. The preparation is where evenings disappear, because every player needs a different card and they all have to be drawn from the same pool of songs.

I have built these by hand and I have built the tool that does it for you, so this is both routes, honestly. Start with the manual one, because it is free and for a single party it is genuinely fine.

## Making music bingo cards by hand

### 1. Pick 40 to 60 songs

Fewer than 40 and the same songs come round too fast, so several people finish at once and the ending is flat. More than 60 and the game runs long, because the odds of any one player completing a card drop off quickly.

Choose songs people will recognise from the first few seconds. A music bingo playlist is not a good playlist: deep cuts you love will kill the room. Aim for choruses people can shout.

### 2. Decide the grid

A 5x5 grid with a free centre square is the classic, and it is 24 songs per card. A 4x4 with 16 songs plays faster and is better for a group that will not sit still for an hour. A 3x3 is a good children's version.

### 3. Build the cards so no two are the same

This is the actual work. Every card needs a different random selection from your song pool, or several people win simultaneously. By hand, that means shuffling your list for each card and taking the first 24, then laying them out in a grid in a spreadsheet or a design tool. For a party of twelve, that is twelve shuffles and twelve layouts.

A spreadsheet helps: put the songs in a column, add a column of random numbers, sort by it, take the top 24, paste into a grid, repeat. It is about five minutes a card once you have the pattern.

### 4. Make a host list

You need your own sheet with every song on it so you can track what has played, and you need to be able to play each song quickly. Scrambling for the next track between rounds is what makes a bingo night drag.

### 5. Print and test one

Print a single card first. Check the text is readable across a table and that the grid lines survive your printer. Then print the rest.

## What usually goes wrong

**Everyone wins at once.** Almost always because the song pool was too small relative to the card size, or because the cards were not properly randomised. If two cards share twenty of twenty-four songs, they will finish within a round of each other.

**Nobody recognises anything.** The playlist was chosen by one person with strong taste. Music bingo is a crowd game and it wants crowd songs.

**The host night becomes a job.** Finding each track, playing it, remembering what has gone, verifying a winning card. That is four things at once while also being at the party.

**Verification is guesswork.** When somebody shouts bingo, you have to check their card against everything that has actually played. On paper, with a room shouting, that is where arguments start.

## FAQ

### How many songs do you need for music bingo?

40 to 60 for a standard 5x5 card. Below 40 the cards overlap so much that several players finish at the same time; above 60 the game runs long because no single card fills up.

### How many songs are on one bingo card?

24 on a 5x5 grid with a free centre square, 16 on a 4x4, 9 on a 3x3. The 4x4 is the one I would pick for a group that wants to play a few rounds rather than one long one.

### How long should each song snippet be?

About 15 to 30 seconds. Long enough that people who half-recognise it get there, short enough that the game keeps moving. If the room is struggling, play the chorus rather than the intro.

### Can you make music bingo cards for free?

Yes, by hand. A spreadsheet, a random sort and a printer will get you there, and for one party that is a perfectly good answer. It costs an evening rather than money.

### How do you check a winning card?

By hand, you compare their marked squares against your list of what has played, which is slow and argumentative in a loud room. Scanning the card instead settles it in a second, which is why the host screen exists.

### What songs work best for music bingo?

Ones with recognisable choruses that most of the room will have heard. Mix decades so nobody's era is missing, and avoid deep cuts even if they are better songs. A bingo playlist is built for recognition, not for listening.
