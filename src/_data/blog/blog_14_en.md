A deck of cards built from somebody's own songs is a good present. Handed over as a stack of cards in a jiffy bag, it looks like a stack of cards in a jiffy bag.

That gap is the whole reason the gift box exists, and it is worth being specific about what it does and does not solve.

## What it is

A foldable presentation box, 12 by 12 cm, printed inside and out, with a window cut into the lid. Behind that window sits an insert card you design, so the outside of the box shows something personal before anybody has opened it.

It holds up to 190 cards and has a divider, which matters more for playing than for giving: a deck that lives loose in a box ends up shuffled into one pile, and the divider is what lets you keep the played cards separate mid-game.

It ships flat as a printed A3 sheet and you fold it yourself along pre-scored lines. No glue, no scissors, about two minutes.

## The window is the part that works

Everything else on the box is packaging. The window is the bit that changes how the gift lands, because it means the person sees something of themselves before they open anything.

What people put behind it, roughly in order of how well it goes down: a photograph of the two of you, a photograph of the event the playlist is about, and a plain design in a colour that means something. Logos work for corporate gifts and nothing else.

One piece of practical advice: pick an image that reads at 8 by 8 cm. A wide group photo where everyone is small looks like a grey rectangle through a window that size. Faces, close up, or a single strong image.

## Where it does not help

If the playlist is generic, the box does not rescue it. A beautifully presented deck of songs chosen at random is still a deck of songs chosen at random, and the people who are delighted by these are the ones who recognise the third card.

Spend the time on the songs first. The presentation is the last ten percent, and it only multiplies something that is already personal.

## Folding it

Do it before you need it, not on the day. It genuinely takes two minutes, but doing anything for the first time while somebody waits is how you crease a lid.

If you are posting it, fold it at the other end or send it flat with the cards separate. A folded box in transit gets crushed corners.

## FAQ

### How many cards fit in the gift box?

Up to 190, with a built-in divider. The divider is genuinely useful during a game, not just for storage: it is how you keep played cards separate from the rest.

### Do I have to assemble it myself?

Yes, and it takes about two minutes. It arrives flat as a printed A3 sheet with pre-scored fold lines, and needs no glue, scissors or tools.

### What should I put in the window?

Something that reads clearly at 8 by 8 cm: a close photograph, a face, or one strong image. Wide group shots disappear at that size. A photo of the event the playlist is about is the version that lands best.

### Can I put my own photo on the box itself?

The box is printed inside and out, and the insert card behind the window is the part you design. For fully custom printed boxes in quantity, that is the business option.

### Is the box worth it if the deck is not personal?

Honestly, less so. The box amplifies a deck that already means something to the person receiving it. If the songs were picked at random, better presentation does not change much.

### Can I order the box separately?

It is an option alongside a deck at checkout rather than a standalone product, since it is sized and designed around QRSong! cards.
