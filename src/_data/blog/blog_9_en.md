If you build a good deck, other people want it. That happens more than I expected, and the discount programme exists because the alternative was people screenshotting their playlist and sending it around manually.

Here is how it works and, more usefully, what makes a playlist other people actually pick.

## How it works

When you make a deck, you can choose to feature the playlist publicly. It then appears on the [playlists page](/[lang]/playlists) where anyone browsing can pick it as the starting point for their own cards.

Every time somebody orders a deck built from your featured playlist, you earn credit against your next order. You are not selling anything and nobody pays you: you get a discount, they get a playlist that somebody actually thought about.

You can turn featuring off at any time, and an unfeatured playlist stops being offered immediately.

## What gets picked

I can see which featured playlists get used and which sit there, and the pattern is clear enough to be worth passing on.

**A specific idea beats a good playlist.** "Songs that were number one the week you were born" gets chosen. "My favourites" does not, no matter how good the songs are. People browsing are looking for a concept they can hand to a room, not for your taste.

**Name it like a shelf label.** The title is the entire pitch, because that is all a browser sees before deciding. "90s one-hit wonders" tells someone exactly what evening they are getting. "Vibes" tells them nothing.

**Build it for recognition.** The same rule as any game deck: choruses and singles, not album tracks. A featured playlist that nobody can guess gets ordered once and never recommended.

**Spread the decades unless the concept is a decade.** A timeline game needs gaps between years to be playable at all.

## The occasions that do well

Decade decks are the reliable ones. After that: weddings, a specific year, songs from films, and country-specific sets that only somebody living there would assemble properly.

The ones that do badly are the very personal ones, which makes sense. A deck built from your own history is the best possible gift for the people in it and a strange purchase for a stranger. Keep those private and put them in a [gift box](/[lang]/gift-box) instead.

## The honest limits

This is a discount on your next order, not an income. If you feature a playlist and nobody picks it, nothing happens and you have lost nothing. If a deck of yours gets popular, your next order is cheaper. That is the whole mechanism, and I would rather describe it plainly than dress it up.

Details and current terms are on the [earn discount page](/[lang]/earn-discount).

## FAQ

### How do I earn a discount on QRSong?

Feature a playlist you have built. When somebody else orders a deck made from it, you earn credit against your next order.

### Do I get paid when someone uses my playlist?

No. You earn credit toward your own next order rather than a payment. It is a discount programme, not a revenue share.

### Can I stop featuring a playlist?

Yes, at any time, and it stops being offered to other people immediately.

### What kind of playlist gets chosen most?

Ones with a clear concept in the title. Decade sets, specific years, wedding decks and film songs do well; anything named after your own taste rarely gets picked, because browsers are looking for an evening they can hand to a room.

### Will people see my name?

The playlist is what is featured, not you. If you would rather keep a deck private, simply do not feature it, and it stays yours.
