Most corporate gifts are forgotten by the following Tuesday. I say that as someone who sells them: the bottle of wine, the branded power bank, the hamper that arrives in December and gets divided up in the kitchen. They are not bad gifts. They are just gifts that carry no information about the people who gave them.

We make custom QR music cards for companies, and I have watched which ones land and which ones politely disappear. The pattern is consistent enough to be worth writing down, whether or not you end up buying anything from me.

## Why a music gift survives the desk drawer

The product is the cards: one per song, a QR code on the front, the artist, title and release year on the back. You scan a card with the free app, a snippet plays without showing the title, and the room guesses. Bingo, a quiz and a timeline game are add-ons that run on the same deck, not separate products. That is the surface. What makes it stick is what is on the cards.

When the songs are generic, it is a nice object. When the songs came from the people in the room, it stops being a corporate gift and becomes a thing about them. Somebody's terrible karaoke song is in there. The track that was playing during the launch. The song three people independently nominated, which turns out to say something about the team that nobody had noticed.

That is the difference between a gift that gets used once and a box that stays on a table.

## The part most people underestimate: let the team choose the songs

We built a voting portal for exactly this, branded in your house style, where your people nominate and vote for the tracks that end up on the cards. You can skip it and send us a playlist, or pick from hundreds of ready-made theme playlists, and plenty of customers do.

But if you are choosing between "fast" and "will actually be remembered", the voting is the part I would keep. It turns the gift into something the recipients helped make, and it does the internal-comms job before the boxes even arrive. The nominating is half the fun.

## What you can actually customise

Four layers, and you can take as many as you want:

- **The cards.** Your logo, photos and colours on every card, front and back. 56×56mm, 350g card with a gloss laminate, so they shuffle like real playing cards rather than like printed paper.
- **The box.** Full print inside and out. Three sizes: 48 cards as a compact thank-you, 96 as the popular middle ground, 192 for something comparable to a retail game on a shelf.
- **The voting portal**, branded, as above.
- **The scan app.** The playback experience can take your colours and logo during the game. Skip it and the cards still play perfectly in the standard app.

On design you have three routes: send print-ready files to our spec, have our in-house team design it, or use a clean unbranded template if budget and speed matter more than branding.

## The practical numbers

- **Minimum order:** 100 boxes.
- **Lead time:** roughly 3 weeks after you approve the digital proof. Not from when you first email: from sign-off.
- **Process:** quote, then you supply artwork and playlist (or we design and your team votes), then a full digital proof, then production, then delivery to one address or split across several.

The lead time is the number people get wrong. If the boxes are for an event, work backwards from the date and leave room for the proof round, because that is where the calendar actually goes.

## One thing that quietly matters: no licensing

Playback runs on each person's own streaming subscription. Nobody has to buy anything, you are not licensing music, and there is no per-seat cost hiding in the model. That is usually the question procurement asks third, and it is the one that kills a lot of otherwise good music-based ideas.

## Where these end up

The obvious one is a year-end relationship gift. ## FAQ

### What is the minimum order for custom music cards?

100 boxes. Below that the setup cost per box stops making sense for either of us.

### How long does a custom order take?

About three weeks from approval of the digital proof to delivery. The stages before that, quoting, artwork and the proof round, depend on how quickly the files and the playlist come back.

### Do recipients need a paid music subscription?

They play through their own streaming account, on any of the supported services. There is no licence to buy and no extra cost attached to the cards themselves.

### Can we use our own designer?

Yes. Deliver print-ready files to our technical spec, PDF for the boxes and PSD for the cards. If you would rather not, our in-house team designs it on a quote basis, and there is a neutral template if you want neither.

### Can our team pick the songs?

That is what the voting portal is for, branded in your house style. You can also just send a Spotify playlist, or choose from our curated theme playlists if you want it done quickly.

### Can you ship to multiple locations?

Yes, either to one address or split across several, which is usually what you want for a distributed team.
